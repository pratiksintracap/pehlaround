How we curate

A proprietary process,before any office sees a company.

Before the Bell's diligence and curation process is the desk's own. Most companies the desk meets never get a room. Those that do carry a Desk Score, and their room holds everything the desk found.

Before a company reaches the desk

  1. Promoter and management meetings
  2. Site, factory and plant visits
  3. Customer orders verified
  4. Customer and supplier feedback
  5. Audited numbers reconciled across sources
  6. Shareholding and title to the shares
  7. Valuation against listed peers
  8. And further steps that are the desk's own

Each room then holds what the desk found: the numbers, the terms and path to listing, the risks in plain words, the documents and the promoter's answers. A new company reaches the desk about every fortnight.

What every office can count on

  • Curated, not listed. A company reaches the desk only after the desk's own work on it, not because it asked to be listed.
  • One Desk Score per company. Set by the desk, dated, and the same for every office.
  • Risks in plain words. Every room states the risks the desk sees, with the source, and they stay up after the deal closes.
  • Two sources disagree? Both are shown. The room says which figure the price rests on.
  • Same room for everyone. Registered offices see one room, one Q&A, one feed. Questions one office asks are answered for all.
  • The desk invests too. SintraCap commits its own capital to every company on the desk, in the same instrument and at the same price as the investors it invites.
The Desk Score

One score per company,set by the desk and dated.

Every company on the desk carries a Desk Score out of 10. It sums up the desk's view of how well the company suits a pre-IPO investment, so an office can compare companies at a glance before opening a room.

What the score weighs

  • Quality of earnings: audited profit, its track record, and how much of it turns into cash
  • Entry price against listed peers today, on trailing and forward multiples
  • Path to listing: the merchant banker, the offer document and how firm the date is
  • Governance and promoters: related parties, the auditor, pledges and title to the shares
  • The business: order visibility, customer spread, growth and the sector
  • Deal terms: the instrument, information rights and protection if the listing slips

Each area is marked against the desk's written checklist and the marks are weighted into one score. The weights and the checklist are the desk's own.

How to read it

  • The bar is 8.0. The score ranks companies that have already been through the desk's diligence. A company that scores below 8.0 is not listed, so every score you see is 8.0 or above.
  • Dated, and the same for everyone. Each room shows the date of its score, and every office sees the same number.
  • It moves when facts move. A filed offer document, new audited numbers or a change in the round can change the score; the room records when it did.
  • Not a recommendation. The score is the desk's view, not a rating of the shares or advice to invest. Read the room, the risks and the documents, and decide on your own advice.
Questions

What investors ask.Answered before you register.

What is the Desk Score and how is it worked out?

A score out of 10 that sums up the desk's view of how well a company suits a pre-IPO investment. It weighs the quality of earnings, the entry price against listed peers, the path to listing, governance, the business and the deal terms, each marked against the desk's checklist. Only companies scoring 8.0 or above are listed. It is not a rating of the shares or a recommendation.

Why don't I see the usual pre-IPO names here?

Because they are on every other platform, priced by the crowd and marketed by every broker; the desk has nothing to add to them. Before the Bell only lists companies it found itself, that no banker has marketed and no platform carries: revenue and profit today, a demonstrable path to listing, and an entry priced below where its listed peers trade. If you can buy it somewhere else, it will not be here.

Who is the desk for?

Three kinds of investor. Family offices: single and multi-family offices and the trusts, HUFs and holding companies they invest through. Funds: AIFs of every category, PMS and other pools with a mandate for unlisted companies. Individual investors who are accredited, or who invest at least ₹25 lakh per company. Registration is open and the desk approves each investor; each declares how it invests and its ticket size, and signs an NDA with each company whose room it opens.

What ticket sizes do deals take?

Each room states its own minimum, usually ₹50 lakh or ₹1 crore. The minimum is set from the round size and the 200-offeree-per-year limit for a private placement, so that a round can fill within the law. Larger allocations can be requested in the room.

Why are company names hidden until I register?

These are private placements. Under the Companies Act an offer of unlisted shares may be made only to identified investors, not advertised to the public, so the site shows each deal as an overview without the name, and opens the details to registered investors.

Does my investment pass through Before the Bell?

No. You pay the company (primary) or the selling shareholder (secondary) directly under the signed documents. Nothing is paid to the desk by the office, on any deal.

What does it cost an investor?

Nothing. Investors pay no fee to register, to open a room, or to commit.

What happens if I pass on a deal?

You tell the desk why, in one line, and it goes into your office's dashboard. The shelf and the alerts learn from it, the promoter never sees your name, and nobody calls to change your mind.

Is Before the Bell a stock exchange or a SEBI-registered intermediary?

No. It is a deal desk operated by SintraCap Advisors LLP, part of the SintraCap family office. It is not a broker, merchant banker or investment adviser and is not registered with SEBI; where a deal needs a registered intermediary, the room names it. It does not run a trading platform, pool investor money or give investment advice. Every investor takes its own decision on the documents provided, with its own advisers.
