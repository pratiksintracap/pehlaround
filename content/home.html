What we look for

Most companies we meet do not make it onto the desk. The ones that do share five things.

  1. Nobody else is selling the round

    No investment bank has marketed it and it is not on the unlisted-share platforms. We usually reach the promoter directly, while the valuation is still reasonable.

  2. Profit, now

    Audited revenue and a positive PAT, or accounts already in audit. We do not take companies whose profits are a forecast.

  3. A credible path to listing

    A main-board or SME listing within about 36 months. The audit, the merchant banker, the DRHP and the listing date are each tracked in the room, so you can see if they slip.

  4. Priced below listed peers

    The entry price sits at a discount to comparable listed companies. Each room shows trailing and forward P/E against the peer set, so you can check the arithmetic yourself.

  5. The promoter stays in control

    The people who built the business keep the majority through the round and after the listing.

Founders can check whether their company is ready to list. It takes two minutes and costs nothing.

Why not an unlisted-share platform?

Most pre-IPO deals still reach investors as a forwarded PDF: a deck three hops from the promoter, numbers that may be a year old, and no way of knowing who else has seen them. The unlisted platforms mostly trade the same forty well-known names, at prices set by whoever is selling.

We work the other way round. We visit the plant, speak to customers and suppliers, and reconcile the numbers before a company gets a room. Every investor sees the same documents, asks questions in writing, and reads the same answers. Allocation requests and passes are recorded against your office, not lost in a chat.

How a round works

For investors, from registering to allotment.

  1. Register

    One form: your name, your office, how you invest. No PAN or ID is needed to look at deals, and there is no fee, now or later.

  2. Sign the company's NDA

    Once your office is approved, you sign an NDA with each company on screen, with your name and a code sent to your email. That company's room opens straight away.

  3. Read the room

    The financials, the terms, the valuation against listed peers, the path to listing, our diligence findings and the risks. Every company's room is laid out the same way.

  4. Meet the promoter

    We set up calls with the promoter and management, and post a written summary. You can also ask questions in writing.

  5. Commit, or pass

    Indicate an amount and we share the draft term sheet and shareholders' agreement. An independent firm can run its own diligence before you sign. Money moves through escrow, and we handle the board resolutions, the offer letter and the return of allotment.
